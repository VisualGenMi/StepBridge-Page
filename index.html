Unifying Trajectory and Distribution Supervision for Efficient Two-Step Diffusion Generation

Move to tilt the rails

Top: each gate is one network evaluation, and each lit span is a transition the student must learn; at two steps one transition spans half the trajectory. Bottom: the target is an interval, not a point. A window of width w opens inside a coarse step, the bar over it is the student's mean velocity across that window, and the teacher returns its velocity at the window centre (violet up: the query; blue down: the answer). The band at the end is the endpoint anchor, the short arc is the four-step checkpoint that initialises the student, and all three rails share one clock.

Teacher — answers, never dictates

Queried at student-visited states, and returns the velocity over a finite window, so the target is an interval mean rather than a single-point slope.

Frozen at four steps; its endpoint distribution is what the student is anchored to, not a per-step target.

Trajectory supervision

A boundary-preserving Pivot path exposes intermediate states inside each coarse interval, while the teacher is queried at the window midpoint. The student matches the teacher's interval-averaged velocity rather than an instantaneous one.

ℒOPD · interval-averaged
2

Endpoint anchoring

The frozen four-step generator yields reference samples; a second estimator tracks the student's own endpoint distribution. Their denoised-prediction difference forms the DMD pseudo-gradient at the student's output.

𝒞DMD · anchored
3

Gradient coordination

A norm controller tunes the effective DMD weight, and asymmetric projection removes only the component of the auxiliary gradient that opposes OPD — leaving the trajectory direction intact.

𝓇total · coordinated

Why not simply add the losses?

Directly summing OPD and DMD drops the VBench Total of the SMD student from 82.40 (anchor alone) to 81.04: the two gradients are actively in conflict. StepBridge resolves this in magnitude and in direction, recovering the gain and then some.

03 Abstract

Two signals, one student.

Trajectory supervision says how to move; distribution matching says where to land. StepBridge uses both — and keeps them from interfering.

Compressing video generation into just two steps requires a student model to learn coherent generation trajectories while preserving the quality of the output distribution under a severely limited sampling budget.1 However, a multi-step teacher provides rich trajectory supervision that may not fully align with the student's compressed sampling process. Conversely, a pre-distilled few-step generator provides a high-quality distributional reference but lacks explicit guidance on the student's generation trajectory.2 To address these challenges, we propose StepBridge, a few-step distillation framework that combines trajectory-level and distribution-level supervision. Specifically, we perform on-policy distillation (OPD) along student-induced proxy trajectories to preserve trajectory-aware learning, while introducing DMD-style endpoint matching against a frozen reference generator to anchor the student's output distribution.3 Furthermore, we design adaptive gradient scaling and asymmetric gradient projection to coordinate the optimization of these complementary supervision signals.4 The frozen reference generator also serves to initialize the student model, while all auxiliary components are discarded after training. Experiments demonstrate consistent improvements in two-step video generation across both purely trajectory-based and purely distribution-based few-step distillation methods, without introducing additional inference overhead.

TL;DR

A frozen four-step generator supplies the endpoint distribution; a frozen multi-step teacher supplies interval-averaged velocity along student-induced trajectories. Adaptive scaling and asymmetric projection keep the two gradients from fighting, so the student improves without changing how it samples at inference.

Detail survives the compression.

107.3°

The OPD and DMD gradients are not merely imbalanced — at the measured probe they point 107.3 degrees apart. Asymmetric projection strips the opposing component while keeping the aligned one, so the update still follows the trajectory objective.

Reading the results

  • Anchoring transfers. SMD, DMD, and DMD2 all improve at two steps, including a method that already performs distribution matching.
  • Diversity is not sacrificed. Cross-seed LPIPS rises for all three students while subject and background consistency stay comparable or improve.
  • No inference tax. The deployed model keeps two DiT evaluations, 1.84 s, and 18.4 GB — exactly the base student's path.
